## The short answer

Rockwell Automation has moved the **Allen-Bradley PLC-5** and **SLC 500** families to end-of-life. If you run these legacy controllers, you have two jobs — often at the same time:

1. **Plan the migration** to the current Logix platform:
   - **PLC-5 → ControlLogix** (large/rack-based, high I/O).
   - **SLC 500 → CompactLogix** (compact/mid-range).
   - Both are programmed in **Studio 5000 Logix Designer** (not RSLogix 5/500), so budget conversion and testing time.
2. **Keep the line running** in the meantime — which means sourcing genuine PLC-5 / SLC 500 CPUs, I/O cards and racks while original stock lasts, without getting burned by counterfeits.

Below: how to choose the migration target, and how we source both legacy spares and the new Logix hardware.

## Why PLC-5 and SLC 500 are being retired

These platforms ran plants for decades, but Rockwell has ended production and is winding down support. The result on the floor: **genuine spares are scarce and getting scarcer**, prices on the grey market climb, and the **counterfeit rate rises** on exactly the cards you're most likely to need in a hurry (processors, analog I/O, comms modules).

That leaves reliability and procurement teams with a familiar squeeze — you can't migrate everything overnight, but you can't afford an unplanned failure with no genuine spare. Managed sourcing bridges that gap.

## Migration targets at a glance

| Legacy platform | Current replacement | Software | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PLC-5** (rack, high I/O) | **ControlLogix** (1756) | Studio 5000 | Conversion tools help, but expect logic review, I/O re-mapping and testing |
| **SLC 500** (compact) | **CompactLogix** (5370/5380) | Studio 5000 | Good fit for mid-range machines; plan wiring/footprint changes |
| SLC 500 (small, budget) | **Micro800 / Connected Components** | CCW | For simpler machines where full Logix is overkill |

> **None of these are drop-in.** Programs migrate from RSLogix into Studio 5000 with conversion utilities, but you must **verify** timers, addressing, and any hardware-specific instructions after conversion.

## Migrate now or source spares first? (how to decide)

**Migrate now when:**
- The controller is critical and a failure would be catastrophic or long to recover.
- You have (or can schedule) engineering time for the Studio 5000 conversion and commissioning.
- You're already modernizing the line, HMI or network.

**Source genuine spares first when:**
- The machine works and there's no migration budget this cycle.
- You need to protect production until a planned outage/turnaround.
- You just need a like-for-like CPU or I/O card to cover a failure.

The trap to avoid: a spare fails, no genuine PLC-5/SLC 500 can be found in time, and you're forced into a rushed, unplanned migration during a line-down event.

## FAQ

**Are the Allen-Bradley PLC-5 and SLC 500 discontinued?**
Yes. Rockwell Automation has moved both families to end-of-life and directs users to the Logix platform (ControlLogix and CompactLogix).

**What replaces the PLC-5?**
ControlLogix (1756) is the standard migration target for rack-based, high-I/O PLC-5 systems, programmed in Studio 5000.

**What replaces the SLC 500?**
CompactLogix is the usual replacement for SLC 500; smaller/simpler machines may suit the Micro800 family.

**Is the migration a drop-in?**
No. Programs convert from RSLogix into Studio 5000 with conversion tools, but you should review and test converted logic, I/O addressing and hardware-specific instructions.

**Can I still buy genuine PLC-5 / SLC 500 parts?**
From remaining and reconditioned stock, yes — but because these are heavily counterfeited, only buy from a source that provides functional testing and traceability.
